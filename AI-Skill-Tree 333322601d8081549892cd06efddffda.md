## Level 1 — Awareness & Recognition (Foundation)

- Recognise that information and content — not all of which is true or accurate — can be shared in a variety of ways by AI systems as well as humans.
- Identify risks of sharing personal data in digital environments, including specific risks in relation to AI systems.
- Identify, in a general way, what is and what is not an AI system.
- Identify common examples of applications of AI systems.
- Recognise that some digital technologies and infrastructures such as AI systems and data centres have large impacts on the environment.
- Recognise the presence of AI systems in digital assistance tools.
- Identify limitations and risks of using virtual assistants and AI systems to support human wellbeing.
- Recognise what AI is in general terms.
- Recognise that AI-generated content should be labelled as such to help others understand its origin and possibilities for further use.

---

## Level 2 — Basic Understanding

- Recognise that while AI systems can generate and integrate digital content, humans are essential to ensure ethical, responsible, and context-appropriate outputs.
- Recognise that it can be difficult to distinguish between information and content generated by humans and AI systems.
- Identify the main features of commonly-used AI-driven and traditional digital search tools.
- Recognise the presence of AI systems in digital collaboration tools.
- Recognise that generative AI is a particular type of AI and is one of various digital technologies that can be used to support content creation.
- Recognise that different kinds of copyright and licences apply to digital content, including AI-generated content, and that these determine how content can be used and shared.
- Recognise the concept of transparent use of AI systems (particularly generative AI) in digital content integration and re-elaboration.
- Acknowledge the essential role of humans in determining how computer programs and AI systems are used.

---

## Level 3 — Comprehension & Description

- Recognise that the data used to train AI systems and how they are trained affects the reliability of the information they provide.
- Recognise that AI systems may produce output which is inaccurate, even if it may seem plausible.
- Recognise that some digital technologies, such as AI systems, may function like a “black box”, making it difficult to explain why or how an output has been produced.
- Recognise that the humans using an AI system are responsible for checking the quality and validity of information and content generated.
- Identify examples of human (cognitive, affective) bias and AI system (data, training) bias in relation to the generation and interpretation of information.
- Describe benefits and limitations of virtual assistants (chatbots) and AI systems in digital communication contexts.
- Recognise the functions, benefits and limitations of AI system functionalities in some digital collaboration tools.
- Identify examples of ethical, responsible and effective human-AI collaboration.
- Describe benefits, limitations and ethical considerations in the use of digital technologies such as AI systems for content creation.
- Describe ethical and transparent practices in the use of AI systems (particularly generative AI) in digital content integration and elaboration.
- Recognise that there are steps that should be followed to develop, validate and deploy a computer program or an AI system.

---

## Level 4 — Application & Interaction

- Critically assess the reliability of sources, information and content in digital environments, considering the role of AI systems, personalisation effects, and commercial or other interests.
- Describe examples of machine learning applications.
- Describe examples of AI system applications from a range of sectors of society.
- Develop and refine questions, commands or statements (prompts) for virtual assistants (chatbots) and AI systems to support non-complex digital interactions.
- Interact with AI systems purposefully, selectively and ethically to support digital content creation.
- Identify examples of legal and ethical challenges relating to copyright in the training of AI models.
- Recognise that machine learning is a branch of AI that enables algorithms to learn from data and make predictions.
- Recognise that AI systems can be used for both cyberattacks and cybersecurity.
- Recognise that regulation of personal data ownership in AI systems is complex.
- Describe privacy implications associated with the use of content shared online, such as to train AI systems.
- Describe strengths, weaknesses and ethical considerations of digital technologies including AI systems in relation to human creativity and problem-solving.

---

## Level 5 — Analysis & Assessment

- Identify examples of how recent and emerging technologies such as AI systems are used in cyberattacks and cybersecurity.
- Describe features of trustworthy digital technologies, such as AI systems.
- Describe methods that can be used to identify deep-fakes.
- Systematically develop and progressively refine questions, commands or statements (prompts) for AI systems to handle complex digital interactions.
- Distinguish between high-risk and prohibited AI systems (according to legislation).
- Describe potential societal, political or economic impacts of prohibited and high-risk AI systems.
- Assess ethical and practical aspects of human-AI collaboration techniques for a given purpose.
- Ensure appropriate and ethical use of digital technologies including AI systems for collaborative tasks.
- Describe ways in which AI systems are used in digital identity management.
- Assess benefits, social and ethical implications of the use of AI systems in digital identity management.
- Describe appropriate and inappropriate uses of AI systems to enhance digital content integration or re-elaboration of complex tasks.
- Identify differences in how ethical and copyright issues apply to training data for AI systems and AI generated content (output).
- Define the concepts and role of human-centric approaches and human oversight in the context of programming and AI systems.
- Describe the main steps in developing, validating and deploying a computer program or an AI system.
- Distinguish between main types of machine learning.
- Identify the main features and purposes of commonly-used machine learning algorithms.
- Identify routine tasks which could be (partially or fully) automated through programming tools or AI systems.
- Assess ethical and practical aspects of the development and deployment of computer programs and AI systems.
- Acknowledge the importance of human oversight and human-centric approaches in the development and deployment of computer programs and AI systems.

---

## Level 6 — Advanced Application & Leadership

- Apply computational thinking, knowledge of programming and/or AI systems to (partially or fully) automate routine tasks.
- Assist others to develop basic programming capabilities and/or capabilities in the application of AI systems to computational thinking tasks.
- Continually scrutinise how AI systems, biases, and various interests shape generation, presentation and interpretation of information and content in digital environments.
- Stay informed about current developments in programming techniques and related applications of AI systems, such as robotics.
- Lead or contribute to complex projects focused on applications of computational thinking, programming or AI systems, including developing, validating and deploying computer programs or AI systems.
- Promote and support proportionate, ethical and effective use of digital technologies, including AI systems, in collaborations.
- Lead or contribute to improvements in or new solutions for human-AI collaboration.
- Promote and support the selective and ethical use of AI systems in content creation.
- Promote and support ethical programming and/or AI systems development practices.
- Apply programming tools or AI systems to complex computational thinking tasks.
